Restaurant Spotlight · Irvine, CA

Spicy City

Location 14310 Culver Dr, Ste A-100
Irvine, CA 92604
Phone (949) 733-9200
CALL RESTAURANT ↗

Real Sichuan Heat on Culver Drive

Spicy City is Irvine's Sichuan spot, and I will be straight with you: the reviews are mixed — 3.5 stars on Yelp, 4.2 on Google — but the food I had earned its place on this list. If you like real Sichuan heat, this is the Culver Drive kitchen to try.

Overview

Spicy City is a single Irvine location on Culver Drive — not the San Diego restaurant of a similar name — and it cooks Sichuan food the way Sichuan food is supposed to taste: bold, numbing, and not shy about the chili. The dining room is casual and busy, the kind of place where the table fills up fast once the food starts landing. I will not oversell it: some reviewers have had uneven visits, and the Yelp rating reflects that. But when the kitchen is on, the flavors are the real thing, and that is why it is here.

Come hungry, come with people, and order family-style. This is not a one-plate kind of meal.

Where to Find It

Spicy City is at 14310 Culver Dr, Ste A-100, Irvine, CA 92604. Reach them at (949) 733-9200.

Hours

  • Monday – Thursday: 11:00 AM – 3:00 PM & 5:00 – 9:00 PM
  • Friday: 10:30 AM – 3:00 PM & 5:00 – 9:00 PM
  • Saturday: 11:00 AM – 3:00 PM & 5:00 – 9:00 PM
  • Sunday: 11:00 AM – 3:00 PM & 5:00 – 8:30 PM

Note: Friday's 10:30 AM opening is from the restaurant's own site; Yelp lists 11:00 AM. The kitchen closes between lunch and dinner, 3:00–5:00 PM daily.

What to Order

Start with the Mongolian Beef — tender beef with onions in a savory, lightly sweet sauce, and the plate I would point a first-timer at. From there, order a spread: stir-fried dishes, greens, and the buns that came with our table, and share everything. The heat builds as you go, so pace yourself and keep the drinks coming. If you are sensitive to spice, say so when you order — the kitchen can guide you, but this is a Sichuan restaurant, and the chili is the point.

Why I Like It

I like Spicy City because it does not water itself down for Irvine. The flavors are assertive, the portions are built for sharing, and it is one of the few places in town where a group can eat Sichuan properly, family-style, without driving to the San Gabriel Valley. I would rather have a restaurant that swings for real heat and sometimes misses than one that plays it safe.

Best Time to Go

Go for lunch or after 5 PM — and do not show up at 4, because the kitchen is closed between seatings. Weekday lunch is the calmest time to try it. Evenings and weekends fill up, so get there when dinner service opens if you want a table without a wait.

Final Thoughts

Spicy City is not for everyone, and I will not pretend otherwise. But if you want Sichuan food with real heat in Irvine, this is where I would send you. Order the Mongolian Beef, bring friends, and share the table.
