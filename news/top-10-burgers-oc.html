Top 10 Burgers in Orange County

My go-to burger list for OC — smash burgers, a gas-station gem, and a dive-bar classic. Every spot here is locally owned.

I get asked about burgers more than almost anything else on OCGrub, so here's my list. These are the ten spots I actually send people to — the places I go back to myself. I kept this one all local: no chains, no franchises, just independent OC spots doing burgers right. Some are smashed thin and crispy, some are stacked a mile high, and one of them is hiding inside a gas station. Let's get into it.

#1 Peter's Gourmade Grill (Tustin)

If you only try one burger from this list, make it this one. Peter's is tucked inside a gas station in Tustin, which sounds like a gimmick until you take a bite — then you get it. The patties are thick, juicy, and seasoned like somebody in the kitchen actually cares, and the builds are big without falling apart on you. I've sent a lot of people here over the years and nobody has ever come back disappointed. This is still the burger I judge every other OC burger against.

What I love most is that it doesn't feel like a place trying to impress you. There's no scene, no waitlist, no influencer wall — just a counter, a grill, and a burger that eats like it costs twice what it does. Come hungry, because the portions are serious, and don't skip the fries. If you're bringing someone to OC for the first time and they ask for one burger, this is where I take them. Every time.

The gas station part is honestly half the fun. You pull in, you walk past the pumps, and then you're eating one of the best burgers in the county — there's something about that that just makes the whole thing better. I like bringing first-timers here specifically for that reaction, the look on their face when they realize the best burger they've had in a while came from a gas station. It never gets old. If you go once, you'll get it, and you'll be the one sending people here next.

#2 Hammer Burger (Santa Ana)

This is my smash burger pick, and it's not close. Hammer smashes them thin so you get those crispy, lacy edges, keeps the menu simple, and just nails the basics — good beef, melted cheese, a soft bun, nothing you don't need. It's the kind of burger you finish and immediately start thinking about ordering a second one. If you're a smash burger person, this is your spot.

The move here is to keep it simple and let the burger do the talking. Get a double if you're actually hungry, because a single disappears fast and you'll be standing there regretting it. It's counter-service, it's fast, and it's the rare place where the hype and the burger actually match. I don't overthink this one — I just go, I order, and I leave happy.

What makes Hammer special to me is the restraint. They could load the menu up with a dozen gimmicky builds, but they don't, and the burger is better for it. Every bite tastes like beef and cheese and bun, exactly like it should, and that crispy edge is the thing I think about on the drive home. It's also a great first stop if someone tells you they don't get the smash burger thing — one bite here and they get it. Trust me on that one.

#3 The Cut Handcrafted Burgers (Irvine)

When I want a burger that feels a little more put-together, I go to The Cut. Everything tastes fresh and made to order, the toppings actually add something instead of just piling up, and you can tell they're using quality ingredients from the first bite. It's gourmet without being fussy about it — you still leave full, you just feel a little fancier about it.

This is my pick for taking someone out when a regular burger joint feels too casual but a steakhouse feels like too much. The menu has enough going on that everyone finds something, the burgers are built with actual care, and the whole place just feels polished without being pretentious. If you're in Irvine and can't decide where to eat, start here.

I like that The Cut works for pretty much any situation. Date night, lunch with a coworker, family dinner where everyone wants something different — it covers all of it without feeling like a compromise. The burgers are the kind you actually slow down for instead of inhaling, which says a lot when I'm usually a eat-it-in-the-car kind of guy. Take your time here. It's built for that.

#4 TK Burgers (Multiple OC Locations)

TK is an OC original, and I mean that as the highest compliment. It's old-school in the best way: charbroiled burgers, crinkle-cut fries, beach-town energy, and prices that don't make you wince. Yes, they have a few locations now, but they're all right here in Orange County and it still feels like a neighborhood spot, not a chain. This is the burger I grew up on, and it holds up.

There's something about TK that takes me straight back — the smell of the charbroiler, the crinkle fries, the whole no-nonsense setup. It's the burger I get when I want comfort, not a project. Grab a classic cheeseburger, get the fries, and eat it like you mean it. No notes.

The best part about TK is that it never tries to be more than it is. No rebrand, no trendy toppings, no fifteen-dollar burger with a fancy name — just a charbroiled burger done right, the way it's been done for years. I respect that more than almost anything in the food world. When I'm having a long day and I just want something that tastes like home, this is where I end up. And the crinkle fries are non-negotiable, don't even think about skipping them.

#5 Mick's Karma Bar (Irvine)

Mick's is one of those places people in Irvine have been quietly gatekeeping for years. It's a small spot in a business park, which makes the line out the door at lunch even funnier — word got out a long time ago. The burgers are creative without getting weird, the Karma Burger is the one I order, and everything comes out hot and fast even when they're slammed. If you work anywhere near Irvine, this is your new lunch spot. Sorry in advance.

Fair warning: get there before the lunch rush or be ready to wait in it, because the line is real and it moves slower than you'd hope. It's worth it, though. The Karma Burger is the move for a first visit — it's the one everyone talks about for a reason — and once you've had it, you'll understand why people who work nearby treat this place like a secret they're tired of keeping.

What I appreciate about Mick's is that it's proof a great burger doesn't need a great location, it just needs to be great. Tucked away in a business park, no big sign screaming at you, and still packed every lunch — that's the kind of place that earned every customer one burger at a time. If you find yourself in Irvine around noon and you see the line, don't walk past it. Get in it. That's how you know you're in the right place.

#6 Burger Parlor (Fullerton)

Downtown Fullerton needed a great burger spot and Burger Parlor is it. The beef is ground fresh, the burgers come out with a proper crust on them, and the whole menu reads like it was built by people who genuinely love burgers — because it was. I like that I can bring anyone here: the purist who wants a classic cheeseburger and the friend who wants something stacked and messy both leave happy.

It's also just a great downtown Fullerton stop. Come for the burger, stay for the walk around the area after — that's the move. The crust on these patties is what keeps me coming back, that proper sear that tells you they care about the cook, not just the toppings. Order whatever sounds good to you. You really can't miss here.

Fullerton has a lot of good food, but Burger Parlor fills a gap I didn't realize was there until it showed up. It's the kind of spot that makes downtown better just by existing — a place you can count on, whether it's a quick lunch or a slow dinner with friends. I like that it works for both. Bring the burger purist, bring the adventurous eater, bring the whole group. Everyone finds their burger here, and that's not something I say lightly.

#7 GD Bro Burger (Santa Ana)

GD Bro started as a food truck, and you can still feel that energy — big flavors, over-the-top builds, zero interest in playing it safe. This is where I send people who tell me they're bored of regular burgers. The portions are generous, the combinations actually work, and it's just fun to eat. Santa Ana keeps delivering, and GD Bro is a big reason why.

If a regular burger feels like a Tuesday and you want a Friday, this is your spot. The builds are bold and the portions don't mess around, so come hungry and bring someone who eats like you do. It's loud, it's fun, and it's the opposite of boring — exactly what a burger should be when you're in the mood for something extra.

The food truck roots show in the best way — everything here feels like it was designed to make you say something out loud when it hits the table. I love that energy, the kind of cooking that isn't afraid to go big. If you're the type who reads a menu and always picks the wildest thing on it, GD Bro was made for you. And if you're not that type yet, one visit here might convert you.

#8 Cassidy's (Newport Beach)

Cassidy's is a dive bar first and a burger legend second — or maybe it's the other way around. The burgers are cooked right behind the bar, they're huge, they're juicy, and there's no pretension anywhere in the building. No fancy toppings, no truffle anything, just a proper old-school bar burger the way they've been doing it for decades. Every OC burger list needs a spot like this on it, and this is ours.

Don't come here expecting a scene or a photoshoot. Come here because you want a big, honest burger and a cold drink in a room that feels like it's been doing this forever. Order it the way they make it, don't overthink the toppings, and enjoy the fact that some things in OC haven't changed — and shouldn't.

There's a comfort in a place like Cassidy's that you can't manufacture. The room has history in it, the regulars have their spots, and the burger tastes like it has for years — because it has. I love that. In a county where restaurants come and go and everything gets a remodel, Cassidy's just keeps doing its thing. Sit at the bar, order the burger, and soak it in. That's the whole experience, and it's a great one.

#9 Bunz Burger Joint (Huntington Beach)

Bunz is where I go when I want it exactly my way. It's build-your-own, so you're in charge — pick your patty, your cheese, your toppings, go classic or go completely off the rails. Their smash burgers are legit too, so even if you don't feel like playing chef, you can't really order wrong here. Easy, casual, and consistently good, which is all I ever ask from a burger joint.

This is the spot I bring the picky eaters and the indecisive friends, because everyone ends up with a burger they actually wanted. My advice: if you're stuck, just get a smash burger with cheese and let them do their thing. And if you do build your own, don't be shy — the whole point is making it yours.

What I like about Bunz is that it takes the pressure off ordering. No wrong answers, no menu anxiety, just your burger, your way, done well. It's also a solid beach-day stop — you're in Huntington Beach, you're hungry after being out, and you want something fast and satisfying without a big production. That's Bunz. Simple, reliable, and exactly what you wanted, because you built it yourself.

#10 Swagyu Burger

I had to close the list with something a little indulgent. Swagyu does wagyu burgers, and yes, you can taste the difference — richer, beefier, with that melt-in-your-mouth thing wagyu does. It's a step up in price from most of this list, but it's not a gimmick; it's a genuinely better bite. Save this one for when you want to treat yourself a little.

Keep it simple here and let the beef carry it. The wagyu is the whole point, so don't bury it under a mountain of toppings — a little cheese, the basics, and you're there. It's the most expensive burger on this list and the one I recommend when someone says "what's the best burger in OC?" and actually means best, not cheapest. Worth it.

I'll be honest, this is my special-occasion burger. Birthday, promotion, a Friday that felt like it earned something — that's a Swagyu day. The richness is real, and you can tell from the first bite that this isn't everyday beef. It melts in a way regular burgers don't, and once you've had it, normal burgers feel a little plain for a day or two. That's the trade-off, and I'm fine with it.

Final Thoughts

That's my ten. What I love about this list is that every single spot is locally owned — these are OC people making OC burgers, and you can taste the difference that makes. Start at the top if you want my personal favorite, jump to Hammer or GD Bro if you're in a smash burger mood, and don't sleep on Cassidy's just because it's a dive bar. That's half the charm. Did I miss your favorite? You know where to find me — I'm always taking recommendations for the next round.

And if you actually work your way through all ten, I want to hear your ranking. Mine changes depending on the day and my mood, but Peter's at #1 hasn't moved in a long time — go see if you agree with me.

One last thing: this list isn't finished, and it never will be. OC keeps opening new burger spots, and I'm always out there trying them. If something new shows up that deserves a place here, I'll update it. For now, though, these ten are the ones I'd put my name on — and I just did.
